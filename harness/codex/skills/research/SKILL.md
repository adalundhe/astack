# Research

Find what is already known, from the most trustworthy sources, before you decide or build.

1. **Frame the question:** the decision it serves, and the answer that would change it.
2. **Search the most trusted sources first** (the trust ladder in **prove-it**): well-cited research and foundational texts; industry-standard references and data; reference and industry-leading implementations, read at their source; engineering reports from teams running at scale, each one workload's data; then the project itself (**rtfm**).
3. **Search in parallel,** a subagent per strand, so the work keeps moving.
4. **Collect the competing implementations** worth building and measuring, each with what it claims and the evidence behind it.
5. **Cite everything:** a link, a title and section, or a file and line. Mark what you verified and what you only read. Say plainly when the evidence is thin or conflicting.
6. **Report briefly:** the answer, the evidence behind it and its rung on the ladder, the competing options, and what is still unknown.
